so since current llmwiki and codegraph are optional mcp , do u recommend me to integrate them at the first day of running my secondary brain , or after a week of running my secondary brain then only implement then , then after two weeks do comparions and metrics analytics to know whether llmwiki or codegraph help me or not

answer:
Your instinct is right — core alone first, then add, then compare. But I'd change one thing: don't turn them on together.

Recommended sequence
Phase	What's running	What you learn
Weeks 1–2	Core brain only	Your real baseline
Weeks 3–4	Core + CodeGraph	Whether CodeGraph earns its place
Later	Core + CodeGraph + LLM Wiki	Whether document knowledge adds anything
Why not both at once
Two reasons, and the second is the one people miss.

They aren't measured the same way. CodeGraph's gate is quantitative — ≥20% fewer code-reading tokens, no accuracy regression, indexing amortized within 20 sessions. LLM Wiki's value is qualitative: did a relevant spec or API doc show up when you needed it? You can't put both on one scoreboard, so running them together doesn't give you one comparison — it gives you two muddled ones.

LLM Wiki has a prerequisite CodeGraph doesn't. CodeGraph is turn-on-and-index. LLM Wiki does nothing until you've fed it a document vault — specs, PDFs, API docs, research. Enable it on day one with an empty vault and you'll measure exactly nothing, then wrongly conclude it doesn't help. Its clock shouldn't start until the vault has content worth querying.

Why CodeGraph first
CodeGraph targets 2.2, one of your five stated pain points, and the token cost there is real and recurring.

LLM Wiki targets none of the original five. It's genuinely useful for external documentation and research, but that was a want you added later, not a pain you started with. Sequence by what hurts.

The thing that could invalidate this whole plan
A baseline only exists if something is recording it.

Comparing week 1 against week 3 requires per-session numbers — context size injected, code-reading tokens, how often the agent still fell back to scanning files. I'm genuinely not certain the running service persists those today. The context compiler counts tokens to enforce its budget, and there's health reporting and structured logging, but whether per-session token usage is written somewhere queryable over weeks is something I'd need to verify rather than assert.

Check that before day one. If it isn't captured, every plan above is unmeasurable and you'd be back to judging by feel — which is exactly how you'd end up keeping a tool because it's popular rather than because it worked.

On baseline length
Two weeks beats one. At 4–8 sessions a day you'd get 30–50 sessions in a week, which sounds like plenty, but session-to-session variance is enormous — a large refactor and a one-line fix are not comparable data points. Two weeks smooths that out. One week is a workable minimum if you're impatient.

What "it worked" should look like
CodeGraph — passes the built-in gate on your repos, and per-worktree indexing cost doesn't annoy you in daily use. Watch that second one; it's where the worktree limitation will show up.
LLM Wiki — you can point at specific sessions where a returned document changed what you did. If you can't name one after two weeks, it isn't earning its place, regardless of how good the vault looks.
And keep in mind the asymmetry: removing either one costs nothing, because neither can hold canonical data. That's what makes this experiment cheap enough to actually run properly.
